Zynga Poker: Hand Clarity

Players thought they had won hands they lost. I redesigned how the table shows a winning hand.

Employer
Zynga
Role
Principal UX Designer
Team
1 product manager, 1 engineer
Scope
Visual exploration, asset creation, implementation
Year
2019

Summary

Problem
Players believed they had won and still lost. They asked for refunds and left 1-star reviews calling the game rigged.
Role
Principal UX designer, with one product manager and one engineer. Visual exploration, assets and implementation.
Solution
Winning cards glow, the rest dim, a stronger banner names the hand, and a kicker card explains ties.
Result
App Store rating back to 4.6, refund calls down 10%. Other experiments ran at the same time.

The problem

Lower ratings mean fewer new installs. We tracked the App Store rating, what reviews said, and customer service calls.

I don't have the number of refund calls. They were frequent enough that the product team was asked to look into them.

Three causes of the confusion:

  • Too fast. The pause between rounds was too short to confirm who had won.
  • Side pots. Side pots and their payouts are hard to understand and hard to show clearly.
  • Hand rankings. Some players didn't know what decides the winner when two players hold the same hand.

Choosing what to fix

I proposed five ideas, chosen for simplicity, engineering feasibility and speed of implementation.

  • Lengthen the pause between hands.
  • Reposition the winner's hole cards so they are easier to see.
  • Show side pot payouts clearly.
  • Dim the community cards that aren't part of the winning hand.
  • Add stronger visual indicators to the winning hand.

I wanted the longer pause. The engineers judged it too technically complex, so it was dropped. The visual changes were quick to build and release.

Exploration

An animated reveal

Hypothesis: an animated reveal pulls the eye to the center of the screen, where the winning cards sit. It also adds time between hands.

When small fixes weren't enough

With every component adjusted, the result still wasn't clear enough. So I pushed the banner further, then explored its colors.

The final design

Side pots

Each player who receives a side pot gets a labeled tag underneath.

New: the kicker card

When two players hold the same hand, the next-highest hole card decides the winner. Showing that card in the banner teaches the rule.

Outcome

App Store rating, restored
4.6
Fewer chip-refund calls
10%
  • Reviews rarely mention incorrect hands any more.
  • Other experiments were running at the same time, so it is hard to attribute any change in business metrics to this update.

Reflection

  • A holdout group on the old table would have separated this change from the others.
  • The pause between hands is the one cause we couldn't address directly.
