Zynga Poker

Leagues, Challenges, the VIP Center and the store, designed during the game's move from native iOS to Unity.

Principal Designer

Overview

I joined Zynga Poker during its transition from native iOS to Unity. I took features large and small from concept to release. Some were led by product managers and others by designers. These are four of them.

Poker Leagues

A league system to raise engagement, which we measured by hands played. Players compete to climb tiers for the status of reaching the top. The higher the league, the fiercer the competition, and highly engaged players are more likely to spend to stay in it.

From requirements to screens

The product owner began with one question: as a player, what would I like to experience? Designers, product managers and engineers brainstormed the stories and their requirements together. I sorted the requirements into groups, and the groups became the screens.

Impact

Leagues moved every KPI we tracked:

  • Retention increased.
  • Session play increased.
  • Hands played increased.
  • Revenue per daily player (ARPDAU) increased.

The follow-up plan was an exclusive top tier, better prizes and more limited-time events.

Poker Challenges

Every game needs objectives. Challenges gives players a tuned set of them, rewards each completed set, and then offers the next.

Impact

Retention rose at 1, 3, 7 and 30 days, and hands played rose with it. Revenue per daily player barely moved. Our theory was that we gave out too many prizes, which is easy to adjust: ask for more effort, or reward less.

VIP Center refresh

The VIP program gives the game's biggest spenders purchase perks and personal support. The refresh added new tiers and spelled the perks out more clearly.

Purchase store

The old store was plain and left no room for new content.

Problems

  • Vertical scrolling on a landscape screen left little room for art.
  • The switch to the Gold page was hard to find, and players contacted support to ask how to buy Gold.
  • Every package needed highlighting, and a narrow row has room for very little of it.
  • Tap areas sat close together, so mis-taps were common.

New design

  • Horizontal scrolling to match the landscape screen.
  • Tabs that also show the wallet balance, with a tab of their own for special promotions.
  • Larger cards for chip and gold packages, with room for bigger art and highlights.
  • Bigger tap areas.

Style guide

Without shared guides the game would have drifted visually. Our team unified its visual language.
