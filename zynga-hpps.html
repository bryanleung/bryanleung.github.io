My role

I led the game's experience design (XD) team and represented design among the senior leads. With the leads of production, product, game design, art, engineering and QA, I agreed what design would deliver for each feature, and held my team to it.

Club Challenge

A social event for clubs: an interactive map with hidden rewards and special encounters.

Club Challenge was a "bold beat", a feature expected to lift revenue by 10% or more over time. The game had about 400k daily players and earned $80k to $90k a day. The aim was to pass $100k.

What I was responsible for

  • Milestones and reviews for the event flow and its edge cases, wireframes, color mocks, Unity implementation and QA.
  • Coverage. I listed every screen and asset the feature needed and assigned each to UI, concept art, animation or sound.
  • Stakeholder meetings to share progress and concerns, set priorities and allocate people.
  • Feedback on UX and visuals throughout.

Choosing a direction

The art director and I set the approach. I proposed two: an immersive 3D maze or a top-down map. Our players are casual, so we went top-down.

The map

In my first concept every node is a puzzle. A regular node is an easy puzzle, a medium node is a mini-boss, and a large node is a boss.

HUD and icons

In check-ins with the UI designer and concept artists, I kept the feedback on what a player would understand. If the objective isn't clear, good-looking art can't make up for it.

Every screen and flow

More than 20 screens, each with its own interaction. We checked every flow and edge case so the whole game loop was covered.

Club Challenge: outcome

  • On time. Rare for a bold beat, and down to precise planning and scoping.
  • Map tools. Game designers can make their own maps with little help from artists and engineers.
  • Mixed revenue. The feature's upkeep didn't justify its cost when smaller features performed better for less.
  • Missed celebration. Defeating the final boss felt underwhelming.

How the team worked

Who does what

In games, UX sets the structure and UI adds the visual layer. Our designers did both, so each of them needed to know where their responsibility began and ended.

Pipeline

The team worked across several time zones, so the process was written down. Design was involved at every stage, from the one-page brief to QA sign-off.

Workflow

Changes get more expensive with time, so we explored widely at the start and answered the big questions first:

  • Do players want this?
  • Will they understand the concept?
  • Is it more fun than the alternative?
  • Can we build it in the time we have?
