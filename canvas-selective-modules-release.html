Selective Modules Release

Making whole modules assignable cut the work of tailoring ten course materials from 150 clicks to 6.

Product
Canvas LMS
Role
Senior Product Designer
Team
1 product manager, 6 engineers
Scope
Design, research, workshops
Year
2024

Summary

Problem
Assigning one material to one student took about 15 clicks across three pages. Instructors avoided it.
Solution
Make the module assignable, so everything inside inherits it.
Result
Ten materials: 150 clicks to 6. A count of steps, not usage data.

The problem

Instructors want to give different students different material: harder practice for an advanced student, or content hidden from a group it doesn't suit.

Canvas allowed it one material at a time, at about 15 clicks across three pages each. Ten materials meant 150 clicks.

It would be great if I could give Modules unique gating rules specific to individual students or groups or sections within the course, similar to how I can create unique assignment due dates for different students and sections.

The harder problem was underneath. An item inside an assigned module can still be assigned on its own, so which setting wins had to be decided before the screens.

Two approaches, tested

I first ran workshops so the team agreed on the instructors' problem. Then I prototyped two ways to assign a material.

  • Permission modal. A new pattern modeled on sharing permissions, and my preference, because the existing interface felt clunky.
  • Assign To tray. The existing component, extended. Safer to build and already familiar.

I tested both in Maze with Canvas Community members. Each got one prototype at random and the same task, assigning an assignment to a student.

How I read the results

  • The tray had the higher success rate and the shorter time.
  • It also had more misclicks, and with 25 and 29 participants a seven-point gap isn't conclusive.
  • The team expected the tray to win. The test didn't contradict that, and didn't prove it.

My preferred pattern showed no advantage worth its cost. I recommended the tray, an existing design system pattern with accessibility already worked out and no one-off components. The engineers confirmed it was cheaper to build.

Design decisions

Assign the module, not each material

Instructors already group materials into modules. Assign the module, and everything inside inherits it. The effort no longer grows with the number of materials.

Keep dates on the material

Due dates and availability differ by material and by student. Supporting them on a module would have been too complex, and instructors confirmed they didn't need it. Assigning a module is only a question of who.

Shorten the path to Assign To

Assign To is now one menu option away on the Modules page and the Summary page, instead of three pages deep.

Rules before screens

The interface changes are small on purpose, so instructors have nothing new to learn. Most of the work was in the rules, such as whether assigning a section overrides a setting for one student in it. I listed the edge cases with a proposal for each, and worked through them with the product manager and engineers. Where there was no evidence, we made a judgment call.

The main rule is that an item's own assignment wins over its module's. An instructor who changes one item by hand meant it, so that change is respected. I proposed this over the alternative, the module overriding its items, and the product manager and engineering manager agreed.

Outcome

Clicks to assign 10 materials
150 to 6
Fewer clicks
96%
  • Before: about 15 clicks per material, ten times.
  • After: six clicks to assign one module holding all ten, to the same students.
  • Not measured: usage. It shipped after I left Instructure. I would track the share of courses that assign a module to specific students or sections.

From instructors, before the feature was enabled:

I can't wait to see this feature enabled! I have been hoping for a feature like this since I started using Canvas back in 2020.

I feel that this will solve a lot of problems in cross listing some of our courses. Teachers will love being able to assign a different module to sections in their course.

Reflection

  • I would set the sample size and the decision bar before the test, so the result could settle the question.
  • Parent and child assignments create many edge cases. I expect we missed a few.
  • A Modules page showing who is assigned to what was out of scope. I think it is the next problem to solve.
