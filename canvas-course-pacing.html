Course Pacing Redesign

Instructors of rolling-enrollment courses can now set a pace for each section and each student. Adoption rose 5% in beta.

Product
Canvas LMS
Role
Senior Designer
Team
1 product manager, 6 engineers
Scope
Design, research
Year
2023

The problem

In a rolling-enrollment course, students start on different dates, so every due date is different for every student. Instructors used to enter those dates by hand, and again whenever a student needed an extension.

The first version of Course Pacing set one pace for the whole course. Instructors told us that without paces for sections and individual students, they couldn't use it.

The feature is essentially useless to me unless I can set paces for my students individually.

The old design had three problems:

  • No pace for a section or an individual student.
  • A dropdown for switching between sections and students, which stops working past about 25 students.
  • Very low usage, which we wanted to raise.

The redesign

We considered several options and chose one that fit the time and technical constraints. It solved the scaling problem and improved the page in other ways:

  • A page title and a one-line description, so instructors know where they are.
  • More prominent stats and default pace.
  • Sections and Students tabs in place of the dropdown.
  • Selecting a section or student opens a full-screen view for setting its pace.

I validated the design with moderated user tests and surveys, and reviewed it with our accessibility engineers for screen reader and keyboard focus issues. During the build I stayed with the engineering team to answer usability and accessibility questions and offer alternatives when something couldn't be built as designed.

Added along the way

  • Blackout dates, so nothing falls due inside a set date range.
  • A way to view and reset pending changes.

Open the Course Pacing prototype in Figma

Results

Lift in adoption during beta
5%

Instructors were glad to be able to set custom paces for their students.

What I learned

  • We couldn't tell whether the redesign or the new features drove the increase.
  • We never set a clear measure of success. More paces created was treated as enough.
  • The first version's design decisions weren't documented. The engineers who built it were the best source.
  • Adjusting the duration of individual items is still inefficient. I wanted to redesign it and met significant resistance.
